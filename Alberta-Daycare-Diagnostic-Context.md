## How Alberta Daycare Funding Works (Plain Language)

There are two completely separate systems depending on the age of the child. Understanding this is critical.

### Children 0 to Kindergarten Age — The Affordability Grant System

This is the main revenue stream for most daycares. Here's how it works:

- The Alberta government signed a federal-provincial agreement to get childcare down to $10/day (target was March 2026, now extended to March 2027)
- As an operator, you sign an **Affordability Grant agreement** with the province
- Once signed, you charge parents a **flat fee**: **$326.25/month full-time** (100+ hours) or **$230/month part-time** (50-99 hours)
- The government pays you the **difference** between that flat fee and what your actual childcare costs are (determined by your region, age group, and program type through "Schedule A" of your agreement)
- Your total revenue per child = parent fee + government grant

**Key risk:** The Canada-Alberta agreement was extended by one year (to March 2027) in December 2025. Beyond that, Alberta has signaled it may not renew under the same terms. This means your funding stability has roughly a one-year runway.

**For-profit vs non-profit matters enormously.** New for-profit programs licensed after March 6, 2026 are **not eligible** for affordability funding. If your existing locations already have signed agreements, you're grandfathered in. If any don't — that's an urgent problem.

> **Official source:** [alberta.ca/affordability-grant](https://www.alberta.ca/affordability-grant)

### Children in Kindergarten to Grade 6 — The Child Care Subsidy

This is a separate, income-tested program:

- For families with children in full-time kindergarten to grade 6 in licensed out-of-school care
- Families earning under $90,000 gross annual income may qualify
- The subsidy is paid **directly to you** (the operator) on the family's behalf
- There's also an **extended hours subsidy**: an extra **$100/month per child** if the child needs 4+ hours outside 6am–6pm or on weekends
- As of April 1, 2025, this subsidy is **no longer available** for children 0 to kindergarten — those kids are covered by the Affordability Grant instead

> **Official source:** [alberta.ca/child-care-subsidy-program](https://www.alberta.ca/child-care-subsidy-program)

## The 9 Funding Programs You Need to Know

Below is every Alberta government funding program relevant to your daycares. Each one has a verified link to the official alberta.ca page. The survey asks you about every single one of these.

### 🟢 ACTIVE PROGRAMS (Currently Accepting)

#### 1. Affordability Grant
- **What:** Monthly operational grant covering the gap between parent fees and your actual childcare costs
- **Who:** Licensed programs caring for children 0 to kindergarten
- **How much:** Varies by region, age group, and program type — defined in your Schedule A
- **Action required:** Sign the agreement, submit monthly claims on time
- **Link:** [alberta.ca/affordability-grant](https://www.alberta.ca/affordability-grant)

#### 2. ECE Workforce Supports Program (Wage Top-Up + PD + Release Time)
- **What:** Four types of funding bundled together:
  - **Wage top-ups** for certified ECEs: **$2.64/hr** (Level 1), **$5.05/hr** (Level 2), **$8.62/hr** (Level 3), up to 181 hours/month
  - **Professional development funding**: up to **$2,000/year per ECE** for approved courses, workshops, conferences
  - **Release time funding**: covers wages for ECEs attending approved training
  - **Mandatory employer contribution**: **9.51%** of wage top-up to offset your CPP, EI, and WCB costs on those top-ups
- **Who:** All licensed programs with certified ECEs (including for-profit programs ineligible for the Affordability Grant)
- **Action required:** Sign the agreement, submit monthly claims identifying each ECE's hours and certification level
- **Common miss:** Operators sign but don't claim every month, or don't claim PD and release time at all
- **Note:** This program replaced the older "Alberta Child Care Grant Funding Program" as of April 1, 2026
- **Link:** [alberta.ca/early-childhood-educator-workforce-supports-program](https://www.alberta.ca/early-childhood-educator-workforce-supports-program)

#### 3. Child Care Subsidy Program (School-Age)
- **What:** Income-tested subsidy for families with children K–Gr 6 in licensed out-of-school care
- **Who:** Paid to the operator on behalf of eligible families
- **How much:** Varies by family income, grade, program type, and hours — families under $90K gross income may qualify
- **Bonus:** Extended hours subsidy of **$100/month per child** for 4+ hours outside 6am–6pm
- **Action required:** Families apply; you receive the funds. Make sure eligible families know about it.
- **Link:** [alberta.ca/child-care-subsidy-program](https://www.alberta.ca/child-care-subsidy-program)

#### 4. Inclusive Child Care (ICC) Program
- **What:** Free supports to help your program care for children with extra support needs
- **What you get:** On-site consultation (up to 6 months), training on inclusive practices (Pyramid Model), and access to funding for engagement supports and resources
- **Who:** Available to **ALL licensed programs** — you don't need to currently serve children with special needs to apply
- **Why it matters for revenue:** If you can serve children with extra needs, you attract families who otherwise can't find care. More enrolled children = more revenue.
- **Action required:** Submit the application form on the alberta.ca page
- **Link:** [alberta.ca/inclusive-child-care-program](https://www.alberta.ca/inclusive-child-care-program)

#### 5. ECE Bursary Program
- **What:** $10,000 bursary per aspiring or advancing ECE for approved ELCC certificate programs
- **How it works:** $5,000 upfront + $5,000 after completion and employment in licensed childcare
- **Who:** Your staff who are uncertified or Level 1 and want to upskill to Level 2
- **Why it matters:** A Level 1 ECE earns you $2.64/hr in wage top-up. A Level 2 earns $5.05/hr. That's an extra $2.41/hr × 181 hours = **$436/month more per person** in government funding
- **Status:** 2025-26 intake closed January 15, 2026. Next intake expected **summer 2026**
- **Administered by:** NorQuest College
- **Link:** [alberta.ca/early-childhood-educator-bursary-program](https://www.alberta.ca/early-childhood-educator-bursary-program)

#### 6. Supplemental / Optional Fees
- **What:** Since the April 2025 funding changes, operators can charge parents **optional fees** on top of the flat parent fee for services like:
  - Hot lunch / meals
  - Transportation
  - Field trips
  - Extended hours care (before 6am, after 6pm, weekends)
  - Overnight care
  - Music, art, language programs
  - Diapers and supplies
  - Extracurricular activities
- **Rules:** Fees must be **optional** (families opt in/out), cannot create preferential access based on ability to pay, and must be reported to the ministry **bi-annually** (October 31 and April 30)
- **Why it matters:** This is a brand-new revenue lever. If you're not charging for meals, field trips, or extended care, you're subsidizing those costs out of your operating budget.
- **Link:** [alberta.ca/affordability-grant](https://www.alberta.ca/affordability-grant) — see **Appendix D** of the Affordability Grant Funding Guide for the full list of permitted optional services

### 🟡 CLOSED PROGRAMS (No Longer Accepting — But Did You Get Anything?)

These programs are closed to new applications, but the survey still asks about them because:

- If you didn't know about them, it tells us you may be missing future rounds

#### 7. Inclusive Spaces Program Grant
- **What:** One-time grants for accessibility renovations (wheelchair ramps, adaptive washrooms) and equipment (sensory tools, assistive technology, culturally relevant materials)
- **Budget:** Up to $15 million over 2024–2026
- **Status:** Final intake closed **November 21, 2025**. Decisions expected early 2026.
- **Link:** [alberta.ca/inclusive-spaces-program-grant](https://www.alberta.ca/inclusive-spaces-program-grant)

#### 8. Building Blocks Capital Grant Program
- **What:** Capital grants for **non-profit and public** providers to build, expand, upgrade or repair facilities
- **How much:** Up to $30,000/space (major capital) or $10,000/space (minor capital)
- **Budget:** Part of $53M federal-provincial commitment
- **Status:** Intake closed **March 15, 2025**. Applicants have been notified.
- **Note:** This was non-profit only. If your locations are for-profit, you were not eligible.
- **Link:** [alberta.ca/building-blocks-capital-grant-program](https://www.alberta.ca/building-blocks-capital-grant-program)

#### 9. Space Creation Grant
- **What:** Per-space grants for creating new licensed spaces in high-need communities
- **How much:** Up to $6,000/space (childcare deserts), $5,500/space (high demand new), $5,000/space (high demand existing)
- **Status:** Closed **November 2024**. No longer accepting applications.
- **Link:** [alberta.ca/space-creation-grant](https://www.alberta.ca/space-creation-grant)

## Important Regulatory Context

A few things to be aware of as we go through this:

- **The Canada-Alberta childcare agreement was extended by one year** (to March 2027) with $1.17 billion in funding. Beyond that, there's genuine uncertainty. Alberta has publicly stated the federal terms are unsustainable.
- **For-profit space creation is capped.** Alberta hit its for-profit space cap of 26,200 in fall 2025 and has allocated an additional 5,000 spaces negotiated in December 2025. New for-profit programs may have limited access to affordability funding going forward.
- **The flat parent fee** of $326.25/month (full-time) and $230/month (part-time) was implemented April 1, 2025. This replaced the previous geared-to-income approach for children 0 to kindergarten.
- **The child care subsidy for children 0 to kindergarten was eliminated** as of April 1, 2025 — those children are now covered by the Affordability Grant system only.
